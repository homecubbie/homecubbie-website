Your house, on your phone, and nowhere else.

HomeCubbie keeps track of a home — what is in it, what needs doing, what is insured and when that runs out. It works with the phone in aeroplane mode, because it never needed the network in the first place.

The promise

There is no account, because there is no server

Most apps in this category ask you to sign up first. HomeCubbie has nothing to sign up to. Everything you enter is stored in a database file on your phone and stays there.

  • No sign-up, no sign-in, no password, no email address
  • No sync, no cloud, no backup we can read
  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no trackers
  • No network requests at all — not even to check for updates

That last one is not a promise about intentions. The app's test suite fails the build if any code, any dependency, or any web address capable of leaving the device is added to it.

The vocabulary

Nine words

The whole app is built out of nine ideas, and it uses the same nine words everywhere — in the menus, in the forms, and here.

  • PropertyThe house. The root of everything.
  • PlaceA room or an area.
  • ThingSomething owned, living in a Place.
  • TaskSomething to do. Needs a schedule to ever surface.
  • PolicyAnything with a term and an expiry — insurance, a warranty, a registration.
  • EventSomething that already happened — a repair, a replacement, a purchase.
  • NoteA fact about the house. No date, no reminder.
  • PersonSomeone dealt with — an insurer, a trade, a household member.
  • AccountSomething billed on a cycle by a provider — electricity, water, internet, council tax.

What that gets you

A house is a hundred small facts you only need once a year

Everything sits where you left it

Places hold Things. A Thing holds its Tasks, its Policies, its history and its files. Nothing is filed twice, so when you open the fridge you get the warranty, the filter schedule and the last repair together.

The boiler service you booked in March

Record it as an Event against the Thing it happened to, with the Person who did it. Next spring, when you are trying to remember who came out and what they charged, it is on the boiler where you left it — not in a drawer, an inbox, or a photo roll.

The warranty that quietly expired

A Policy has a term and an end date, and HomeCubbie tells you before it runs out rather than after. The same goes for the buildings insurance, the car registration and the boiler cover. Reminders are scheduled with your phone, so they arrive with no server anywhere in the loop.

The alarm code nobody can remember

A Note holds a fact about the house with no date attached. Sensitive ones stay hidden until you tap to reveal, the app can be locked behind Face ID or your device passcode, and the database is encrypted by the operating system whenever the phone is locked.

Your data leaves only when you carry it out

HomeCubbie can write a backup file of everything you have entered and hand it to the share sheet, where you decide what happens to it. That is the one and only way data moves, and you start it every time.

The reverse is also true, and worth saying plainly: because there is no server, there is nothing to restore from if you lose the phone and have no backup file. Take one occasionally, and put it somewhere you trust.
